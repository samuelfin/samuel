**SAMUEL**

Financial Intelligence

**PROJECT OVERVIEW, REQUIREMENTS & TEAM PLAN**

A proposed digital platform for financial news, Nigerian capital-market
data, company research, public-offer intelligence and AI-assisted
financial understanding.

**Prepared for:** Project leadership and financial subject-matter review

**Technical lead:** Full-Stack Software Developer

**Status:** Concept and MVP planning --- not a final business, legal or
investment recommendation

# 1. Executive overview

Samuel Financial Intelligence is proposed as a digital financial-media
and market-research platform. Its first market focus would be Nigeria,
helping investors, professionals, businesses and financially curious
readers understand the Nigerian capital market and wider economy. The
longer-term ambition is to expand across African markets and, where
commercially and legally appropriate, international financial coverage.

The product should combine the editorial experience of a high-quality
financial news publication with useful market data and research tools.
It should not begin by trying to replicate Bloomberg Terminal or become
a brokerage. The first release should establish trust, reliable
sourcing, useful company research and a consistent publishing operation.

# 2. Product vision and intended users

The platform should serve several audiences without assuming every
visitor is an experienced investor:

- New and retail investors who need accessible explanations, offer
  information and company background.

- Active investors and market watchers who need company updates, price
  context, results and watchlists.

- Financial professionals, analysts and researchers who need organised
  source material and comparable data.

- Business owners, executives, journalists and students who need
  dependable financial and economic context.

Core promise: reliable financial information, presented with context, so
people can understand the market better. Clearly distinguish factual
reporting, historical data, analyst opinion, AI-generated summaries and
educational content.

# 4. What the first version should include

The MVP should be deliberately narrow. It should prove that users find
the information useful and that the team can publish accurate, sourced
material consistently before expensive live-data, multimedia and AI
features are added.

- Professional responsive homepage with editorial stories and a market
  snapshot.

- Selected NGX-listed company pages, beginning with companies users most
  frequently research.

- Company announcements, annual/interim reports and key financial
  figures with reporting-period labels.

- IPO and public-offer information area using official issuer and
  regulator documents.

- Basic watchlist and simple alert preferences.

- Editorial workflow for drafting, financial review, fact-checking and
  publication.

- Limited AI report explainer that links important answers to source
  documents and pages/sections.

- Disclosure system showing source, retrieval time, reporting period and
  whether data is delayed, end-of-day, estimated or demo.

# 9. Compliance, trust and risk controls

The platform\'s legal position must be reviewed before it publishes
securities analysis, provides recommendations, promotes offers, accepts
payment for investment-related services or routes users into
transactions. Nigeria\'s Investments and Securities Act 2025 is now the
relevant primary statute. Its definition of an investment adviser
includes a person registered to advise on securities or issue, publish
analysis or reports concerning securities. The exact application to the
proposed editorial, research, AI and commercial model requires advice
from a Nigerian capital-markets lawyer and, where appropriate,
clarification from the SEC.

- Begin with factual financial information, general education and
  sourced reporting while counsel assesses the activity boundary.

- Do not accept customer investment funds, execute trades, custody
  assets or provide personalised investment recommendations in the MVP.

- Do not market an IPO/public offer as an invitation to invest without
  checking issuer, intermediary and SEC requirements.

- Use disclaimers, corrections policies, conflicts-of-interest
  disclosures and separation between editorial and sponsored material.

- Keep audit trails of source documents, data changes, editorial
  approvals, AI outputs and publication timestamps.

- Protect user data, apply least-privilege access and establish
  security-incident and user-complaint processes.

This is a planning warning, not a legal opinion. Calling a feature
\'information\' or \'AI research\' does not automatically remove it from
regulation.

# 10. Commercial model to test

- Free access: selected market summaries, basic company profiles, public
  education and a limited number of articles.

- Premium individual subscription: deeper company research, historical
  comparisons, saved alerts, downloadable research and enhanced AI
  document tools---only where data licences permit.

- Institutional services: team workspaces, research tools, licensed data
  access or APIs, subject to data and regulatory agreements.

- Media and sponsorship: clearly labelled sponsorships, events,
  newsletters and video partnerships with editorial-independence
  safeguards.

Do not set final subscription prices until user interviews, competitor
research, vendor costs and willingness-to-pay tests are complete. Data
licensing and editorial staffing may be major recurring cost drivers.

# 12. Budget categories to approve

A reliable budget should be prepared after scope and vendor discussions.
Price these cost headings rather than guessing:

- Company formation, legal advice, regulatory assessment and compliance
  support.

- NGX or authorised vendor data subscription, display/redistribution
  licence and historical data access.

- Cloud hosting, database, object storage, backups, monitoring, email
  and security services.

- Software development and design time; contracted data engineering or
  QA support.

- Financial analyst/editorial research capacity and fact-checking.

- Video/audio production, editing, hosting and media rights.

- AI model usage, document processing and evaluation.

- Marketing, user research, customer support and business development.

# 14. Brand and naming assessment

Samuel Financial Intelligence is a credible working name. \'Financial
Intelligence\' communicates a broad information and research ambition,
while \'Samuel\' gives the brand a distinctive identity. The name can
support expansion beyond Nigerian equities into macroeconomics,
companies, fixed income, commodities, African markets and global
financial coverage.

The main weakness is category ambiguity: in some contexts, \'financial
intelligence\' refers to anti-financial-crime, fraud or
anti-money-laundering intelligence. The brand therefore needs a
consistent descriptor and tagline that make its market-research and
financial-media purpose immediately clear.
