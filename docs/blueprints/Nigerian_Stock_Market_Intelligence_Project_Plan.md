# 1. Executive Summary

The proposed project is a Nigerian Stock Market Intelligence Platform: a
web-based product focused on investment information, market analysis,
company research, IPO tracking and investor education. Its purpose is to
make reliable financial information easier to find, understand and
compare, particularly for everyday Nigerian investors who may not have
the time or expertise to interpret market data and company filings.

The product should initially operate as an information, research and
education platform---not as a stockbroker, exchange, investment manager
or trading application. It can explain market activity and present
factual analysis while directing users to appropriately authorised
providers when they want to transact.

+-----------------------------------------------------------------------+
| **CORE PRODUCT THESIS\**                                              |
| Help people move from hearing about an investment opportunity to      |
| understanding the company, the numbers, the risks and the available   |
| official information.                                                 |
+=======================================================================+

## Initial strategic recommendation

- Start with Nigerian Exchange (NGX) listed equities, company
  intelligence, market news and IPO/public-offer tracking.

- Make company research and plain-English explanations the product\'s
  strongest differentiators.

- Use AI to summarise and explain source documents, while keeping every
  important claim traceable to its source.

- Expand later into bonds, treasury bills, mutual funds, other Nigerian
  markets and eventually selected African markets.

- Validate data rights, licensing boundaries, user demand and
  willingness to pay before building advanced features.

# 2. The Opportunity in Nigeria

Interest in public offers, listed companies and investment products can
bring new people into the capital market. However, awareness does not
automatically mean understanding. Investors may hear about a public
offer or a company's share-price movement without knowing how to assess
the business, read its financial statements, compare its valuation or
identify the risks.

## Problems the platform could address

- Investment information is spread across exchange notices, company
  announcements, financial statements, news sites and broker platforms.

- Financial reports can be difficult for new investors to interpret.

- Investors may struggle to compare companies in the same sector using
  consistent metrics.

- IPO and public-offer information can be time-sensitive and requires
  careful checking against official documents.

- Market headlines often describe what happened without explaining why
  it matters or what information is still uncertain.

## A practical user need

A user who hears that a company is offering shares should be able to
find the offer details, issuer background, official prospectus, key
financial information, relevant dates, risks, and links to the
authorised subscription channel---without the platform pretending to
guarantee returns or telling the user what they personally should buy.

# 3. IPOs and Public Offers as a Use Case

A high-profile public offer, such as the Dangote-related offer discussed
during the brainstorming session, illustrates why accessible investment
information matters. It can attract attention from experienced investors
and people encountering the capital market for the first time.

## What the platform should show for each IPO/public offer

- Issuer and offer overview: who is raising capital and what the
  business does.

- Offer structure: offer type, price, number of shares or units, minimum
  application and other terms as stated in official documents.

- Timeline: opening date, closing date, allotment information and other
  key dates, clearly labelled and updated.

- Official documents: prospectus, offer circular, exchange notices and
  regulator announcements.

- Company fundamentals: revenue, profit, debt, cash flow and other
  relevant historical financial information.

- Risks: business, sector, regulatory, currency, execution and market
  risks disclosed in the source documents.

- How to participate: links to official or authorised channels, not
  collection of user funds by the platform.

- Post-offer updates: listing status, announcements and available market
  performance data.

+-----------------------------------------------------------------------+
| **IMPORTANT\**                                                        |
| Do not imply that an IPO is automatically a good investment. The      |
| platform should explain the offer and its risks neutrally,            |
| distinguish facts from analysis, and make official source documents   |
| easy to access.                                                       |
+=======================================================================+

## Example user journey

1.  A user hears about a listed bank or a new public offer.

2.  They search for the company on the platform.

3.  They see a plain-language company overview and the latest available
    market information.

4.  They review financial performance, dividends, announcements and peer
    comparisons.

5.  They read a source-linked AI explanation of the latest financial
    report.

6.  They save the company to a watchlist or open the official documents.

7.  If they decide to invest, they are directed to an authorised broker
    or offer channel.

# 7. AI-Powered Financial Intelligence

AI could become a major feature, but it should operate as a research
assistant---not an unquestionable investment authority. The platform
should retrieve information from trusted documents and datasets, produce
explanations, and show users where the information came from.

## Potential AI capabilities

- Financial statement summarisation: explain revenue, profit, assets,
  liabilities, cash flow and notable changes.

- Company comparison: compare two or more companies using the same
  reporting periods and clearly defined metrics.

- Announcement explanation: summarise what a corporate action or
  exchange notice says and why it may matter.

- IPO document assistant: answer questions from a prospectus, with page
  or section references.

- Market briefings: create a daily summary of notable market movements
  and announcements.

- Financial terminology assistant: explain concepts such as P/E ratio,
  earnings per share, dividend yield and market capitalisation.

## AI safeguards

- Use retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) over approved source
  documents rather than relying only on model memory.

- Cite the source document and relevant page, table or section for
  material claims.

- Display reporting period and publication date; never mix figures from
  different periods without saying so.

- Label estimates, assumptions, derived calculations and missing
  information.

- Do not fabricate prices, financial figures, forecasts or company
  announcements.

- Include clear limitations and avoid personalised buy/sell
  recommendations unless the appropriate regulatory requirements are
  met.

# 9. Regulatory and Trust Considerations in Nigeria

This project sits close to a regulated financial-services environment.
The initial scope should be deliberately limited to information,
education and research until the regulatory position is reviewed by a
qualified Nigerian capital-markets lawyer.

## Initial lower-risk product boundary to investigate

- Publish factual market information where the platform has the right to
  use and redistribute the data.

- Explain publicly available company information and financial
  disclosures.

- Provide educational material and general analytical tools.

- Link users to official exchange, issuer, regulator and authorised
  provider pages.

- Avoid receiving investment money, executing trades, holding client
  assets or managing portfolios.

- Avoid personalised recommendations or promises of returns without
  first obtaining legal advice on applicable licensing requirements.

## Key legal and compliance workstreams

- Review the Investments and Securities Act 2025 and current SEC Nigeria
  rules, circulars and guidance.

- Obtain legal advice on whether any planned research, advisory, data
  distribution or intermediary activity requires SEC registration or
  another approval.

- Confirm NGX and other data-provider licensing, redistribution and
  display rights before publishing prices or derived data.

- Create editorial standards, correction processes, risk disclosures and
  a complaints contact.

- Protect user accounts and personal information, and review applicable
  Nigerian data-protection requirements.

| **TRUST PRINCIPLE\**                                                  |
| Never present delayed data as live data. Label timestamps, data       |
| sources, coverage limitations and any missing values clearly.         |

## Suggested high-level architecture

Official sources and licensed data providers → ingestion and validation
jobs → structured database and document storage → analytics and
calculation services → AI retrieval and explanation layer → web
dashboard, search, watchlists and alerts.

The AI layer should not be the system of record. Verified data and
source documents should remain the authority; AI should interpret them
and make the information easier to use.

## Recommended MVP

The first release should be small enough to build and validate, but
useful enough to solve a real problem.

- A clean market overview with clearly labelled data timestamps.

- A searchable directory of selected NGX-listed companies.

- Company profile pages with official filings and announcements.

- A basic financial performance view using verified source data.

- An IPO and public-offer information page with official documents and
  dates.

- A beginner-friendly education section.

- A basic AI report explainer for a limited set of source documents,
  with citations.

# 13. Design and User Experience Direction

The interface should feel credible, calm and information-rich without
overwhelming beginners. A professional financial-terminal influence can
guide the information hierarchy, while the overall experience should
remain simpler and more approachable than a traditional trading
terminal.

- Use a restrained palette: deep navy or charcoal, white/off-white
  surfaces and one teal or green accent.

- Prioritise readable numbers, clear labels, consistent date formats and
  visible data timestamps.

- Use tables for detailed financial data and charts only where they make
  trends easier to understand.

- Provide plain-language definitions for financial terms.

- Make mobile layouts a first-class requirement; many Nigerian users
  will access the platform by phone.

- Separate factual data, editorial commentary, AI-generated explanations
  and user-entered portfolio information.

# 15. Research and Validation Plan

Before committing to a full build, conduct a short discovery sprint.

8.  Interview 10--15 people: new investors, active retail investors,
    finance students, analysts and brokers.

9.  Ask what information they look for before investing and where they
    currently find it.

10. Ask them to walk through how they researched a company or public
    offer recently.

11. Identify which part is most frustrating: finding data, understanding
    reports, comparing companies or tracking events.

12. Show a simple prototype and observe whether users can answer a
    practical research question.

13. Test whether users would pay for advanced analysis, alerts or
    AI-assisted reports.

14. Speak with data providers and a capital-markets lawyer before
    finalising the feature scope.
